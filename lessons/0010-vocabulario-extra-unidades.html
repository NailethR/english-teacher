1. Unit 1A — Comida y verbos de acción/no-acción

Verbos de "no-acción" (nonaction/stative) describen estados, no acciones: feel, believe, belong, know, want, like, love, taste, smell, sound, own. Casi nunca van en -ing, aunque describan algo "en este momento".
❌ I'm loving this song → ✅ I love this song.
❌ Something is smelling good → ✅ Something smells good.

Ejercicio A — Simple Present o Continuous

  1. I sometimes tired after lunch. (feel)
  2. Can you call back later? We a party right now. (have = acción social, sí admite continuo)
  3. I him. (believe = estado)
  4. Something good. What are you making? (smell = estado sensorial)
  5. That cake delicious. Did you make it? (look = estado de apariencia)
  6. I all kinds of vegetables. (love = estado)

2. Unit 2B — Strong adjectives

Un "strong adjective" es una versión intensificada de un adjetivo común — ya significa "muy X", así que nunca se usa "very" con ellos (se usa absolutely en su lugar). very tired → exhausted (no "very exhausted").

3. Unit 3A — Transporte, comparativos vs. superlativos, "as...as"

Ejercicio B — Vocabulario de transporte (compound nouns)

  1. Don't forget to put your ______ belt on. →
  2. Sorry we're late. There was a terrible ______ jam downtown. →
  3. We need to fill up at the ______ station. →
  4. The traffic is always worse during ______ hour. →
  5. Slow down! There are ______ cameras on this road. →

as + adjetivo + as = igualdad ("tan X como"). not as ... as = desigualdad ("no tan X como"). No confundir con comparativo (-er / more).

Ejercicio C — comparativo, superlativo o as...as

  1. Gas isn't as expensive in the US it is in the UK.
  2. My father drives more slowly my mother.
  3. This is cheapest gas station in the city.
  4. I think trains are dangerous than cars. (menos peligrosos)
  5. You're at the Sheraton? We're staying at the same hotel you.
  6. Why don't we walk? It's the expensive way to travel.
💬 El "as...as" y los comparativos de inferioridad (less/least) no estaban en el temario oficial pero son fáciles de confundir con el comparativo normal — avísame si necesitas más ejemplos.
